# Product value over public technical knowledge

WS-08, Product Landscape. Primary sources retrieved **2026-09-23**.

## Finding and scope

**INFERRED:** Public knowledge does not eliminate product value, but the defensible unit is rarely "the knowledge" alone. The inspected offers charge for operated execution, maintained implementations, private-code capacity, organizational controls, support, or differentiated analysis. These mechanisms can coexist with public rules. They do **not** establish demand for a new portable domain-package format.

**OPINION:** Test a maintained native transformation operator plus an accepted-change workflow before building a registry or general modernization platform. The useful incremental result would be a change that a customer can authorize, execute, inspect and accept with less total effort at equal quality. Neither a package nor an agent-generated success message is that result.

**Strongest precedent: Moderne.** It puts multi-repository execution, semantic implementation and private-repository licensing directly around executable transformation recipes. It is also an incumbent alternative, not merely an encouraging analogy. **Weakest analogy: Datadog's operational-data advantage transferred to episodic migration.** Continuous telemetry and sparse migration outcomes have very different economics and opportunities for reuse.

The [machine-readable model](../structured/product-value-patterns.json) contains ten bounded cases, seven ordered patterns, source locators, hypotheses, proposed rejection tests and explicit unknowns. It distinguishes:

- **DOCUMENTED:** A primary source specifies a license, feature, billing boundary or financial disclosure.
- **INFERRED:** A plausible buyer, value mechanism or comparison derived from those facts.
- **HYPOTHESIS:** A defensibility claim or testable product proposition.
- **OPINION:** The recommended order of experiments.
- **OBSERVED:** No product execution, purchasing behavior, productivity uplift or migration outcome was observed in this workstream. Retrieval is not product validation.

A published paid offer is evidence of an offer, **not** observed willingness to pay, realized feature revenue, profit or a moat. Datadog reports FY2025 total revenue of $3.43 billion [DD4]; that demonstrates an operating commercial business, not revenue attributable to OpenTelemetry or a data-network effect. All ten cases' attributable-revenue fields are `null`. External documents are mutable snapshots, not archived raw evidence. Current license and pricing details must be rechecked before procurement; no raw artifacts or original notes were changed.

### Why the license distinctions matter

**INFERRED:** This landscape does not support "everything underneath is open, so only orchestration matters." Terraform core, Sonar's inspected Java analyzer, Moderne's proprietary recipe subset, JetBrains' unified distribution and JFrog CE all complicate that claim. Conversely, their restrictions do not prove that *secret technical facts* create the value. Public concepts, proprietary implementations, licensed executable rules, operated services and customer-private context are separate inputs.

**DOCUMENTED contradictions preserved:** HCP Terraform's overview prose says Free includes SSO/policy enforcement, while its feature tables exclude them; separate tables also disagree on Essentials SSO. Its undisputed resource-hour model and Premium audit/custom RBAC are sufficient for this comparison. Sonar's billing introduction describes organization-level billing for Team/Enterprise, while the detailed Enterprise section describes enterprise-level annual licensing. The model records the latter and the conflict. Neither discrepancy is silently harmonized into certainty.

### Does the mechanism require proprietary domain knowledge?

**INFERRED, mechanism-specific, not whole-product claims:**

- **Not required for the selected mechanism:** GH execution, TF coordination, DD operated telemetry, JF storage/promotion. Their paid implementations, operational labor and customer-private state still matter.
- **Not demonstrated as necessary:** RH maintenance/support and JB integrated tooling. Public concepts do not imply inexpensive release engineering or good UX. Incident expertise may differentiate without being proven exclusive.
- **Mixed:** SN combines public inputs and proprietary research; SO combines capacity/governance with differentiated, license-constrained analyzers; MO combines open recipes/semantic execution with proprietary recipes. No evidence isolates how much buyers pay for each.
- **Unknown:** DM conversion internals and exclusive knowledge contribution. Its documented free conversion offer cannot settle the question.

Private source code, artifacts, dependency graphs, infrastructure state and telemetry are customer-specific inputs. They are **not automatically vendor-owned domain knowledge**, transferable training data or legitimate authority over target choice. Neither source availability nor a signed artifact establishes correctness, and correctness does not itself grant permission to execute.

## Prioritized product-value patterns

**OPINION:** This is an order for a narrow Modernize-adjacent experiment, not a market-size ranking. It weights task similarity, a documented paid boundary, measurable incremental benefit and dependence on data actually available. Each pattern has a corresponding hypothesis/test in the JSON.

| Priority | Pattern and evidence | Implication and falsifier |
|---|---|---|
| P1 | **Fleet execution and integrated change workflow**: MO private execution/LST reuse, GH execution, TF coordination, JB tooling. [MO3, MO5, GH1, TF1, JB2] | Compare accepted change cost against native recipes plus existing CI/review. Reject an added layer if setup, review/rework and upkeep are not lower at equal acceptance quality or no incremental buyer exists. |
| P2 | **Scoped validation and accountable governance**: DM row checks, SO enterprise audit, TF controls, GH approval, JF promotion. [DM2, SO1, TF1, GH3, JF1] | Separate permission, evidence and check coverage. Reject if evidence merely duplicates existing controls, misses material failures or increases review without improving decisions. |
| P3 | **Maintained compatibility and support**: RH subscription services, SN curation, JB maintained tooling, MO recipe distribution. [RH1, RH2, SN4, JB2, MO4] | Test a maintained native operator through two real version changes and an escalation. Reject if upkeep/support cost exceeds credible coverage revenue or demand is only episodic. |
| P4 | **Distribution and workflow access**: GH repository gates, JB unified IDE, JF artifact delivery, DM cloud adjacency. Modernize documents CLI/IDE handoff. [GH3, JB1, JF1, DM1, MS1] | Existing developer reach can favor incumbents. Compare activation/procurement burden for the same capability without changing target options. Reject if distribution adds no advantage or imposes unacceptable restrictions. |
| P5 | **Hosted capacity or private managed execution**: GH/DM/DD consumption, JF SaaS versus self-managed, MO private licensing. [GH1, DM1, DD1, JF2, MO5] | Operations are a potential product, not free infrastructure. Compare all-in managed versus existing-CI/customer-controlled delivery. Reject if no operational advantage or margin survives support and data constraints. |
| P6 | **Curated intelligence with demonstrable incremental quality**: SN research, SO analyzers, MO proprietary recipes. [SN4, SO3, MO4] | Knowledge may still differentiate. Ablate enrichment while holding implementation/workflow/checks fixed. Reject exclusivity as the value explanation if public inputs achieve equivalent net results. |
| P7 | **Customer operational history, not an assumed data flywheel**: DD telemetry, TF state, MO repository context/local license checks. [DD1-DD3, TF1, MO3, MO5] | Test held-out same-customer and cross-customer lift separately, only with explicit reuse permission. Reject a cross-customer moat if data is unavailable, unrepresentative or provides no transferable lift. Local evidence can remain useful. |

Trust is an outcome to earn, not a feature label. "Certified" should mean a named scope, issuer, procedure, period and obligation. This work did not inspect an independent certification report, warranty coverage, insurer loss data or a contractual guarantee of migration correctness. Vendor support, audit logs and SOC claims cannot substitute for those.

## Comparison with the Modernize value hypothesis

The existing [provider-influence analysis](../docs/product-analysis/provider-influence.md) proposes expertise, integration, orchestration, validation, UX/trust/governance, distribution and operational learning as value layers. The evidence changes their relative strength:

1. **Integration and execution remain credible, but not generic orchestration as a moat.** Moderne is the closest product comparator and existing alternative. Its LST implementation and repository coordination do substantial work; a thin wrapper or new manifest is not equivalent. GH/TF show that a paid capacity boundary can coexist with free integration features.
2. **Validation is promising only when bounded and independently enforced.** DMS distinguishes not-enabled, pending, suspended, no-primary-key, mismatch and validated states. That is a stronger design lesson than a generic "validated" claim. Approval authority, origin verification, rule evaluation and business acceptance must remain distinct.
3. **Provider expertise may be useful without authorizing provider selection.** DMS documents free conversion alongside paid capacity. A cloud-consumption incentive is a plausible explanation, not disclosed subsidy economics. It creates a competing zero-priced planning/conversion offer; it does not prove bias in a specific Modernize run.
4. **Distribution favors an incumbent at least as much as a new business.** Modernize's documented CLI/IDE layers and use of OpenRewrite/custom skills [MS1] already overlap P1/P4. Documentation is positioning, not proof that every recommendation is transparent, every step validated or every workflow operational. No Modernize-specific pricing or attributed revenue is established here.
5. **Operational learning is the weakest untested claim.** Modernization outcomes can contain proprietary code, inconsistent acceptance labels and selection effects. More collected data does not necessarily improve generalization. Moderne's documented private/local license flow also shows that paid delivery need not require centralized telemetry.
6. **Do not overcorrect into "knowledge has no value."** Proprietary research and recipes are explicit in SN/MO, and analyzer implementation remains differentiated in SO/JB. Public domain concepts, exclusive facts and high-quality executable encodings are different. The thesis needs an ablation, not a slogan.

**INFERRED:** Durable value may live in an accountable, maintained implementation at the boundary between public knowledge and a customer's authorized change. That is compatible with an agent being a client of the domain. It does not require a novel domain-package abstraction, demonstrate that models/harnesses are interchangeable, or prove that an independent vendor can capture enough of the savings to survive.

## Proposed next experiment and decision

**HYPOTHESIS; not executed:** Recruit an actual modernization owner, an engineering operator and the person who accepts operational risk. Select one repeatable framework/dependency change with independently specified acceptance criteria. Use the same repository set, target, context, review rules and allowed actions in both arms:

- **Baseline:** Native recipe/tool plus the organization's existing CI, issue tracking and review.
- **Candidate:** The same transformation plus only the proposed fleet coordination, maintained coverage and explicit acceptance/evidence record.

Predeclare check coverage and unresolved states; preserve authorization separately from recommendations. Record setup, machine capacity, all retries, reviewer time, rework, support, maintenance and accepted outputs, including failures. Use counterbalanced tasks where feasible so learning order does not favor the candidate. An independent reviewer checks seeded and naturally occurring failures.

Obtain a **budget owner's concrete commitment** for the incremental service and a second-use scenario; enthusiasm or a vendor price list is not a purchase. Carry the operator through two real upstream changes before generalizing upkeep economics. A cheaper accepted change does not by itself establish a viable vendor margin: include acquisition, support, deployment/privacy constraints and recurring maintenance.

**OPINION:** Continue only if the incremental layer improves acceptance effort/quality and has a buyer beyond what incumbents already provide. If a native recipe plus current workflow is adequate, keep the knowledge in native form and stop the platform/package expansion. Governance may justify a narrow evidence/authorization integration even when transformation intelligence does not; measure it separately.
